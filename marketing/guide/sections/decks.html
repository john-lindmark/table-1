Decks and cubes

Sections

Decks have Main deck, Sideboard and Considering, and Commander and Oathbreaker decks a command zone. Cubes have Main and Considering. Considering is ideas: it never counts as missing.

Organized

Decks start grouped by type (creatures, instants, lands…), cubes by color; View → Group by switches either to type, color, color identity (each color combination, Azorius to Five-color) or mana value (0 to 7+, then lands). Inside each group, cards go by name, or by mana value or set from View → Sort. The grid and stacks keep the same groups, and the sideboard shows its own cards.

Missing

Each line says what you own and what's missing, and the Decks list shows the total. Any copy of the card covers a line New: any printing, finish or language. Copies of the printing and finish the line names count first, then your other copies of that card, line by line; one copy covers one line. "Owned N" on a row counts the card in any printing; a printing's own row, opened under a card with several, counts that printing. Basic lands are never missing: their lines just say how many you own, and in Grid and Stacks New they never get the red short mark.

Building and editing

  • New deck / New cube: name it, pick a format, then add cards. Or import a list, or (decks) find one online. A deck can also be made from a deck photo's decklist New: Add to my decks on the decklist saves it as a private deck (see After the event).
  • Command zone New: pick Commander or Oathbreaker for a new deck and choose its command zone right there. A Commander deck takes a commander (a legendary creature or planeswalker) and, if it can have one, a partner or background. An Oathbreaker deck takes an oathbreaker (a planeswalker) and a signature spell (an instant or sorcery). Each goes in as the card's original printing. On the deck's screen, Command zone sits above the cards in every view, as two cards side by side (commander and partner, or oathbreaker and signature spell): tap a card for Open card, Change or Remove, or an empty slot to choose one. A change is one edit in the version history. An imported deck's commander shows there too. Oathbreaker is new as a format; Table 1 doesn't check Oathbreaker legality yet.
  • Name New: a deck's or cube's Edit has a Name row; Change renames it (trimmed, up to 60 characters). The new name shows everywhere at once and the lists re-sort.
  • Format New: a deck's or cube's Edit has a Format row; pick another format and the deck keeps its cards. Switching a deck to Commander or Oathbreaker brings up its command zone to fill.
  • In this deck New: choosing a commander (or partner, oathbreaker or signature spell) from the deck's screen lists the deck's own cards that fit first, then every card. Tapping one of the deck's own moves that copy in, out of the main deck or sideboard; replacing a commander sends the old one back to the main deck. In Commander and Oathbreaker decks, Edit's Move to and a card's Section offer the command zone too.
  • Partners New: the partner slot appears only when the commander can have one (Partner, Partner with, a partner group such as Partner—Father & son, Friends forever, Choose a Background, Doctor's companion, or a Time Lord Doctor), and its search offers only cards that pair: another card with partner (or the same group, or Friends forever), the card named by Partner with (the search opens on it), a Background, the Doctor, or a Doctor's companion. The empty slot says which. Until a device has card data from 29 October or later, any legendary creature or Background can go there, as before.
  • Who sees this sits at the top of every deck, cube and binder: Private, Friends or Anyone. New decks start Private, so a brew never leaks.
  • Add, remove or replace works like Cube Cobra's: an Add box (the catalog, Scryfall syntax, or Scan) and a Remove or replace box (only the list's own cards) on one screen. Pick a card in either. With only a card to remove, the button removes one copy; with both picked it reads Replace and swaps that copy for the new card in the same section, as one edit. The + on a search result adds a card at once.
  • Printings, collapsed: a card with several printings or finishes in a section is one row (total, "3 printings", what's missing) with a chevron that opens each line; View → Expand all printings opens them all. Edit mode always shows every line.
  • Search inside the list hides sections with no match; stats, Missing and legality still count the whole list.
  • Edit mode: steppers on every line; tap a line to move it between sections, remove it, or change its printing or finish.
  • Split finishes: on the printing screen choose how many copies to change, then the printing and finish. Moving 2 of 4 to foil leaves 2 non-foil and 2 foil as separate lines.
  • Languages New: each line has a language as well as a printing and finish, so 3 English and 1 Japanese copy of a card are two lines. A line takes its language from the copy that went in (from your collection, a scan or a printing that exists only in one language); since 1 November the card's page from a deck has no Language cell. Lines in another language show it ("JA") in the list, edit mode and version history. Cards added from search start in English unless the printing exists only in another language; a card added from your collection keeps its language; scanned cards go in as the language they were read as. Changing a line's printing keeps its language, unless the new printing has its own. Missing counts your copies in any language. Exports and shared links don't carry the language yet.
  • Conditions New: each line also has a condition (NM, LP, MP, HP, DMG), so 2 near-mint and 1 lightly played copy are two lines. A copy added from your collection or a binder keeps its condition, and scanned cards take the one picked in the scanner; anything else starts near mint. Rows, edit mode, version history and Change printing show it when it isn't near mint ("LP"). Nothing on the deck picks it: the card's page from a deck shows no Condition. Changing the printing, Match collection, moving sections and Sync with Cube Cobra keep it. Exports and shared links don't carry it yet.
  • Duplicate a deck or cube from Edit mode; the copy starts its own history. Delete removes it from every device.
  • Export the current version (see Importing and exporting lists), or make a deck image.
  • Buy New: under Add to collection, "12 to buy · $83.40" opens the Buy screen: skip what you own, exact printings or one cart, tokens and boards, the total, then TCGplayer.

Sideboard guide New

A plan for each matchup: what comes in, what goes out, and notes. Sideboard guide sits under Stats on every deck except Commander and Oathbreaker ("3 matchups · sideboard 15").

  • Matchups: + adds one, picked from the format's Insights archetypes by share (when Insights covers the format) or typed (up to 40 characters). Each row reads "IN 3 · OUT 3" or "NO CHANGES", with Check when something's off. A matchup's Edit renames it, moves it up or down, or deletes it. Up to 40 matchups.
  • In and Out: a matchup opens on two cells, In and Out, and its plan below them ("+2 Fury", "−1 Ragavan"). In lists your sideboard, then Considering, each with − and + up to the copies in the deck; Out lists the main deck. Cards stay in the order you added them. Ins needn't match outs: the rules let you change the deck's size as long as it stays legal.
  • Cards past 15: the In screen's search adds any card to Considering and brings one in, so you can try more than 15 cards while you work the guide out. The Sideboard screen lists Sideboard and Considering with "In 4 matchups" on each card; tap one to move a copy (or all) to the other section.
  • What it checks: a card no longer in the deck, more copies than you have, copies coming from Considering ("not printed until it's in the sideboard"), and the deck going under 60 cards after sideboarding (100 for Canadian Highlander; Other isn't checked). The guide screen also says when the sideboard is over 15 in a Constructed format.
  • Notes per matchup, up to 1,000 characters: play or draw, what to watch for, what to keep.
  • Print: the print icon opens the page as it will print, then Print (the phone's print sheet, or the browser's). White paper in Table 1's mono type with uppercase labels and thin boxes, the teal only as the line under the header; matchups in two columns, each box kept whole, then your sideboard. Only what you can bring in at a tournament is printed: cards still in Considering, and cards no longer in the deck, are left off and counted above the preview. Under the header: "MTR 2.11 · Between games only · Remove from the play area before each game" (the rules allow brief notes made before the match, read between games). It warns past two pages ("a sheet or two") and when the sideboard has more than 15 cards. A guide on your phone is fine at Regular REL; at Competitive REL bring it printed.
  • The guide saves with the deck, syncs to your devices and works offline. Only you see it: shared decks and links don't include it.

In the meta New

Under Stats, each of your decks in an Insights format (Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, Vintage, Pauper, Canadian Highlander) shows the archetype it plays as, by the same rules Insights uses.

  • The archetype with its win rate over the last 30 days ("Win 53.3%": matches against other archetypes in every event Insights reads, draws counting as not won; shown from 20 matches) and its change on last week ("▲ 2.1 pts on last week": this week's win rate against the week before's, when both have 20 matches; "Same as last week" when flat).
  • Good against and Bad against: its three best and three worst matchups over 90 days, one a line ("Boros Energy 58%"), each from at least 20 decided matches.
  • How similar New: the archetype row reads "88% similar" on its own line above the win rate: the deck against the archetype's 10 closest lists of the last 90 days (the year when there are none), each the share of copies the two have in common out of the larger deck, basic lands and the sideboard left out, averaged over the ten. A deck copied from a recent list reads 100%.
  • Brews New: a deck Spice calls a Brew (50 or more) shows no In the meta rows; the archetype's numbers say little about a deck that plays mostly other cards.
  • Tap any of the rows to open the archetype's Insights page over the deck; Back returns to the deck New (on iPhone from its next build). A deck no archetype rule names reads "In the meta · Doesn't match an archetype Insights knows"; decks in other formats, and roto decks, show nothing.
  • On the website since 30 October; on iPhone from build 40 (build 39 had the rows but never loaded them); Android with its next build.

Add to collection New

Built a deck or cube you now own in paper? Add to collection on its screen says how many copies of its exact printings you're missing and opens a screen to add them.

  • Missing adds only what your collection lacks of the exact printing, finish and language the list names New: a copy you own in another printing, finish or language doesn't count here, so a deck listing a Japanese foil Ragavan adds one even if you own an English one (basic lands are never missing, so it leaves them out). That differs on purpose from the deck's own "missing", which counts any copy of the card; Every card adds every copy the list holds, basics included, for when you've bought or opened another set of it.
  • Each card goes in as the printing, finish, language and condition the list records New. A deck's Sideboard switch decides whether its sideboard comes too; Considering is never added.
  • There's no condition or language to pick: each copy goes in as the condition its line records New (near mint unless it came from your collection or a scan with another condition) and the line's language. With Missing, copies you own in any condition count, those in the line's own condition first. The screen lists every card it will add with its count and language.
  • It all goes in as one change: Undo takes it back out.
  • From one card: a card opened from a deck or cube has Add to collection in its details (the list icon): add the copies you're missing of the listed printings (by the same exact rule, in each line's condition and language), every copy the list holds, or one copy of a listed printing. A basic land never has copies missing, so its row reads "Basic land · add the copies listed here" and offers every copy or one.
  • Roto decks don't offer it: their cards belong to the cube.

Sync with Cube Cobra New

A cube you keep on Cube Cobra too can be brought up to date from there. Sync with Cube Cobra sits on every cube of your own (not roto decks), under Export, and says which Cube Cobra cube it follows ("cubecobra.com · lindmark").

  • The link: a cube imported from a Cube Cobra link remembers it. A cube imported before 30 October, or built by hand, asks for the link the first time and remembers it after. Change link points it at another cube.
  • What changed: a summary ("2 in · 2 out · 1 new printing", or "Up to date"), then In (on Cube Cobra, not in your cube), Out (in your cube, not on Cube Cobra) and New printing or finish (the same card in another printing or finish: "2ED #266 → LEA #265", "Non-foil → Foil"). Main and Considering are compared on their own: a card moved from one to the other is Out of one and In the other. Tap a card for its page. Cards Cube Cobra lists that aren't in the card data are named and left out, as on import.
  • Languages stay as you set them: Cube Cobra doesn't record languages, so a Japanese Mana Drain that Cube Cobra still lists stays Japanese. A new printing that exists only in one language takes that language.
  • Accept changes saves the whole cube as one new version, "Synced with Cube Cobra"; Restore in Version history puts the old one back. Keep as it is goes back without changing anything.
  • If the cube changed on another device between checking and accepting, nothing is saved: it says so and offers Check again.

Version history

  • Every change is kept. Edits within 10 minutes of each other become one version, so a run of +1s is one entry.
  • Open any version to see what was added and removed; Restore makes it current again as a new version. Nothing is ever overwritten.
