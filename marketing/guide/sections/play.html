Playing in an event Players

  • The Events tab's live card shows your table, opponent and round clock, and leads with reporting your result (or opening the life tracker, if you chose that in settings).
  • Report a match in one tap; your opponent confirms or disputes it.
  • Sign up by link New: before round 1 an event's link (table-1.site/s/…) shows when and where it is, the format, who runs it, the rounds ("Auto rounds · Top 8"), how many have signed up ("12/16 players · 3 on the waitlist") and Sign up. Signed out, Sign up goes through sign-in (Apple, Google or email, which make an account if you don't have one) and comes back signed up, in the app or on the website. A full event offers Join the waitlist, then says your place ("You're number 2 on the waitlist"); when a place opens you're seated and get a "You're in" notification. Private events don't show.
  • Leave event New: until round 1, leave your seat or your place on the waitlist from the event screen; the next person on the waitlist moves up.
  • Decklist New (Constructed events that take them): the Decklist row on the event screen ("Add your decklist", or "Required · add it before round 1"; Events says "Add your decklist" too while it's missing). Paste it as text (Arena, MTGO and Moxfield lists all read, and the screen counts "60 main · 15 sideboard" as you go) or pick one of your own decks in the event's format. Change or remove it until round 1; after that ask the organizer. Only you and the organizer see it.
  • The link after round 1 New: the same link shows Standings (with the top 8 bracket once there is one) and Matchups: every round's tables and scores, newest first. Anyone with the link can follow along, signed in or not. In a draft with pods New, each round's matchups come pod by pod ("Round 2 · now · Pod 1", then Across pods); the apps' event page does the same.
  • Nearby (Events home, on all three; the website since 30 October New): open events within 150 m, nearest first, with Join, after the note that your name shows in its pairings and standings. Your location is sent once per look and never kept.
  • Event code New (iPhone and Android Events tab, under Scan QR and Nearby; the website's box on its home page): type the organizer's 4-character code and Join, or Paste what you copied (a code, a join link, the QR's link, or a message with the code in it) and it joins at once. A wrong, closed or full event says so under the box.
  • Join by QR, link, code or nearby. A join link (with or without the QR's secret) opens the app and joins when the app is installed, and the website otherwise. A nearby event you've already joined (or run) says Joined and opens the event instead of offering Join.
  • No account yet? Scan the organizer's QR with your phone's camera. Signed out, the page (or the app, if you have it) names the event you're joining (its name, format, venue, organizer and how many players are in) and offers Continue with Apple, Google or email. Each one makes your account if you don't have one; a new email account adds your first and last name, and then you're seated. On the website you can also join as a guest with just a name. Private events show only their code until you're in.
  • Get the app New: after joining on a phone's browser, the event screen offers the app for that phone and says which sign-in to use there ("Continue with Apple", or the email address). Your seat is on your account, so signing in the same way puts the event on the app's Events tab. On Android the store link also carries the event through the install: the app opens on it the first time (after you sign in). Not now hides it on that browser. On iPhone it opens the TestFlight beta; on Android it appears once the app is on Google Play. Guests don't see it (their seat lives in that browser).
  • Before you join, the app says plainly that your name shows in the event's pairings and standings, to its players and anyone with its standings link.
  • Standings and matchups are public by link, so friends can follow along. A code join to a full event puts you on the waitlist (guests are told it's full); the QR and nearby joins say it's full, since the organizer can add you.
  • In a top 8, your playoff rounds are named Quarterfinals, Semifinals and Final, and can't be reported as a draw.
  • On iPhone, the round shows in a Live Activity and widgets (the round clock, your table and your opponent on the Lock Screen; results go in from the app) New, and push notifications tell you when you're paired.
  • In drafts you see your seat at the table and the pass direction for each pack; in team events your team and seat letter.
  • Once your last match is in New: the event screen opens on Add a deck photo, under a line saying why (Last match in, Event over or Dropped), and goes straight to Take a photo or Choose from photos for your deck. Done means the event is over, you dropped, or your last match's result is in (either player's report counts): the last Swiss round with no top 8, a lost playoff match or the final; with a top 8, once the playoffs start without you. With a photo added it's a Deck photo · Added row that opens them. Ten minutes after you're done, with no photo up yet, a notification says so once New (see Notifications).
  • Deck photo: photograph your own deck (or pick a photo) with a note. Before saving, Rotate left or Rotate right New turns it upright. Only you and the organizer see it until the event is over. On iPhone, Android and the website, when you're signed in; a guest's deck can be added by the organizer. The photo is read into a decklist New within about half a minute: the Decklist row under the photo opens it, to correct and to add to your decks (see After the event).
  • Find an event New: public events, and your friends' Friends events, are listed under Find events on the Events tab (see Finding events). The first time you sign up for a public or league event, the app says once that results in public events are public: standings, league points and the format boards.
  • About New: when the organizer wrote a description or an address, an About row on the event's page opens it, starting with the rules level and the decklist deadline when they're set. An event in a league has a row with the league and season, which opens the league.
  • Sign up a team, or join one New (team events whose organizer switched on Teams sign up): on the event's page, Sign up a team asks for its name, signs you up and makes you captain in seat A. Join a team takes the team's code (type it or Paste), or open the link your captain shared and the code is filled in; you get the next free seat. Your team then shows the name, the members by seat, how full it is ("2/3") and the code with Copy and Share. The captain can move members up and down the seats, Make captain, Remove a member and Rename team, until round 1. Anyone can Leave team: if the captain leaves, the next member takes over, and an empty team closes. Everyone sees Teams, each with "2/3" or COMPLETE.
  • Team Trios New: your team, the pairings and your own match show your seat and its format ("A · MODERN"). You play the other team's player in the same seat.
  • Call a judge New: on your match on the event screen, and in the life tracker's Options. It asks first, then tells the organizers your table. While the call is open the screen reads JUDGE CALLED, with Cancel to take it back. Guests can call one too.
  • Extra time New: when the organizer gives your table more time, "+5:00" shows beside the round clock on the event screen and in the life tracker; your table's time runs that much past the round's.
  • With a top 4 New, your playoff rounds are the Semifinals and the Final.
  • An event you're not in New (signed in, in the apps): its link opens the event page in the app, never the website: the sign-up page before round 1, then Standings | Matchups (top 8, teams and pods included; every round's tables and scores), refreshing every 20 seconds. Its notifications open the same page.
  • Follow New: on that page, for anyone signed in who isn't its organizer or a player (and can see it by its link). It's listed on the Events tab under Following (running first, then upcoming, then finished in the last week), with a notification when each round is paired, when it finishes and if it's cancelled. Following → turn notifications off, or Unfollow. Up to 200; nobody sees who follows.
  • On the website, the event screen has a back arrow to Events (or wherever you came from). So does the event's shared link page New (sign-up, standings and matchups, signed in or out): opened straight from a link, the arrow goes to the home page.
