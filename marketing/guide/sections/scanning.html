Card scanning iOS and Android

Point the camera at a card, or a stack you flip through. Recognition happens on the phone against the downloaded card file: no photo leaves the device and nothing waits on the network.

Which card

The camera reads only the card inside the outline on screen: of the cards it can see, the one nearest the outline's centre, so cards lying around it are ignored. It reads that card's text and matches the name to the catalog, handling double-faced cards, names cut off at the edge, and a misread letter or so. Tokens and art cards aren't scanned.

Split cards, and cards held turned New: when most of the text in view reads sideways (a split card held upright) or upside down, the scanner reads the lines in the text's own direction, title first. Every half of a split card (Fire // Ice, fuse and Aftermath cards, Rooms) names it: the first half as before, a later half (Ice, Memory) when it's read whole and no other card has that name, and any two halves read anywhere on the card. Upright cards read as before.

Which printing

The set code and collector number at the bottom pick the printing (the copyright year and prerelease date stamps help); otherwise the original printing. A card with no readable English name can match by set and number alone.

Old cards New: Alpha, Beta, Unlimited and Revised print no set, number or year, so the scanner guesses the most printed, Revised (then Unlimited, Beta, Alpha), and a foreign one its Foreign Black Border printing. An artist line with no copyright year tells it the card is from 1993–94 (4th Edition on prints a year).

The border New: when nothing printed names the set, the scanner reads the card's border color from the camera picture (a few brightness points around the card; on Android along the outline). Black goes in as Beta, white as Revised, a French name with a black border as Foreign Black Border. A border it can't tell (dim light, a sleeve) falls back to Revised, and your fix still sets the next guess. On 58 old-frame photos: iPhone 47 right, 1 wrong; Android 31 right, none wrong (the rest unknown).

Your fix is the next guess New: change a scanned card's printing and the next card the scanner can't tell apart goes in as that set, nearest that collector number. Fix one Beta card and a Beta stack follows; fix one retro fetchland and the rest come in retro. Anything the camera does read still wins.

Frame and artist New: the card file carries each printing's illustrator and frame. An "Illus." credit means a 1993 or 1997 frame, old cards and retro-frame reprints alike, so a retro Misty Rainforest goes in as MH2's even when only its name, rules and credit were read; an artist after a brush means a 2003-or-newer frame; and the artist's name read (a letter or two off is fine) tells variants of one set apart. The number at the end of a copyright line ("© 2024 Wizards of the Coast 407"), lone numbers on cards without power and toughness, and more spellings of the set code count too, and what the last frames of the same card read is remembered, so a set code in one frame and the number in another pick the printing together. What no text tells is left: The List reprints, prerelease stamps the camera can't read, Unlimited from Revised.

When it counts

A card counts once its name holds steady: 0.2 s when two frames read the same exact printing New, 0.4 s when one did, 0.8 s without (and read at least three times). A second of misreads doesn't start it over (it was half a second, which kept old cards from ever counting). It can't count again until it has left the frame for a second, so holding it still never adds two.

The whole frame New (iPhone): the camera reads the whole frame, as it did before 28 October. From 28 October to build 43 it read only around the outline and read the card's bottom strip on its own, deskewed and enlarged; a card held larger than the outline lost its title, and the extra image work slowed the phone, so both are gone since build 44.

Never from the flavor text New: a card's name is only ever taken from above its type line. Flavor text names people who are cards too (an Ice Age Counterspell whose title didn't read went in as Gustha Ebbasdotter), and that can't happen now, on iPhone and Android.

Steady speed New: the scanner remembers only the last ten frames of the card in view (about two seconds), checks each artist's name against them once per frame, and skips a camera frame when the last one is still being matched, so a long session on a busy desk runs as fast as its first frame. (From 28 to 31 October it kept every frame and slowed as it went.) And on iPhone the scanner screen no longer redraws on every camera frame New: until build 42 each frame redrew the whole screen, scanned rows included, so the longer the list, the slower each frame, until the scanner was closed and reopened. From build 43 a frame redraws nothing.

The screen stays on New while the camera is scanning, so a long stack never locks the phone halfway. Changing a row's printing or language, or closing the scanner, lets it dim again.

The button stays in reach New (Android): the scanner's Add button sits above the navigation bar on every phone; on Android 15 and later it had slipped partly under it.

Any language, no setting

  • Cards in German, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Japanese, Korean and Chinese match by their printed name. Nobody picks a language.
  • The copy goes in as the language the card says. A language code next to the set code ("WAR • JP") decides it; otherwise the language of the name the camera read, so a card read by its German name goes in as German. An English name doesn't say, since many cards print the English name in every language, so those go in as the language you add new copies in. When a name reads the same in two languages (often Spanish and Portuguese once accents are dropped), your new-copies language wins if it's one of the two.
  • The camera reads Latin and Cyrillic first. When the text in view names no card for 0.6 s it switches to reading Chinese, Japanese and Korean, stays there while cards match, and switches back after 1.2 s of misses.
  • On Android New: Japanese, Chinese and Korean are three readers, one a frame in turn. The reader that names a card stays; a frame read before a switch counts for the reader that read it; every reader gets a frame before it goes back to Latin; and the next switch starts with the reader that named the last card. The three language models come from Google Play services: the app asks for them at install and again when the scanner opens if they're missing (until they arrive those scripts can't be read).

Where scanning is used

  • The Collection bar New: the camera at the top of the Collection tab scans a pile, then Add opens Add to over the scanner for every card at once: Collection, Deck (then its section), Cube, Binder, Want (any printing, these printings, or these printings and finishes; one want per card) or Trade (new or open; Get, or Give when you own every card). The camera takes nothing while the list is open, and closing it goes back to scanning with the cards kept. A pick adds everything in one go, each card as its row shows it (printing, finish, condition, language), closes the scanner, and the Collection tab says what went where, with Undo for the collection, a binder, a deck or a cube.
  • Prices New: every scanner shows each card's price on its own line under the row: one copy in its finish and condition ("$9.00"; "≈ $7,992.55" when it's Cardmarket's converted; "No price" when there's none), and the bar adds the total ("12 scanned · $83.40"). Prices load in the background, so with no signal the rows just have no price line.
  • Add cards: everything scanned goes in as one batch when you tap Done, and one Undo takes it all back. The cards show in your collection (and binder) straight away, without waiting for the server New.
  • Decks and cubes (Add, remove or replace): scanned cards go into the section you picked as one edit, in the printing and finish the scanner read.
  • Binders: scanned cards go in new, straight into the binder. Wants: each scanned card becomes a want.
  • Trades: cards you give match only cards (and printings) you own, preferring copies in a for-trade binder; cards you get match any card.
  • Redraft piles after a set draft.
  • Each scanned row shows its language (the one the card says, else the one you add new copies in); tap it to change it. When you give cards in a trade, the scan keeps the language of the copy you own.
  • The camera can't tell foil, so each scanned row has a finish toggle. Tap a row to change its printing; rows marked "Check printing" are the ones it wasn't sure of.
  • Back asks first New: with cards scanned, the back arrow (or Android's Back) asks "Add the N cards you scanned?" with Add N, Leave without adding and Keep scanning; no card is taken while it asks. With nothing scanned it just closes.

Learning from corrections

Nobody is asked to report a bad scan; what people already do says it. Changing a scanned row's printing, deleting a row and adding the right card by hand, or adding a card by hand after the scanner couldn't read it (backing out with nothing taken, or a couple of seconds of a card it couldn't name since the last one it took) each send a small report: the text the camera read (never an image), what it picked and what the right card was. Reports go in the background and can be turned off in Account → Settings → Improve scanning.
